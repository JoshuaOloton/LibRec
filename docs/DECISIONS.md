| 2026-10-13 | **Relevance in evaluation (was Q4):** a held-out item is relevant only if it is a real RATE with rating ≥ 4. Simulated borrows stay in training but never count as relevant. Held-out books that are not relevant (rated < 4, or borrow-only) remain in the candidate pool, so recommending them counts as a miss. Patrons with no relevant held-out book are left out of the metrics for that seed (precision and recall are undefined for them); the number evaluated is reported per seed | Simulated borrows were generated from each patron's genre preferences, so counting them as relevant would bias the comparison towards content-based filtering |
| 2026-10-13 | **Top-N for evaluation (was Q5):** N = 10 | Common default; a 1,000-book catalogue always has 10 candidates, so Precision@10 divides by 10 |
| 2026-10-13 | Evaluation split (`lib/evaluation/split.ts`): partition whole (user, book) pairs, so a book's BORROW and RATE rows always land on the same side. Per patron, hold out `max(1, round(0.2 × distinct books))` books, capped so at least 2 distinct books stay in training (a patron with 3 books holds out 1). Randomness is a mulberry32 stream seeded per run, over patrons sorted by id and books sorted by id, so a seed reproduces the split exactly. Seeds 1–5 | Per-pair partition rather than per-interaction avoids leaking a held-out book through its paired borrow. All configurations are run on the identical partition |
| 2026-10-13 | **Cold-start evaluation segment** = patrons with fewer than `coldStartThreshold` (3) **distinct books in training** after the split; the rest form the "warm" segment. (The switching rule in Cycle 4 will count the same thing, so the segment and the rule agree) | Counting distinct books keeps a paired borrow + rating on one book from counting as two signals. Every patron keeps ≥ 2 books, so the cold segment is the patrons with exactly 2 |
| 2026-10-13 | **Borrow weight in the content profile = 3.5.** The profile is the mean of the TF-IDF vectors of the patron's books, weighted by the rating (1–5). A book with a real RATE uses that rating; a book with only a BORROW uses 3.5. One entry per distinct book, so a borrow-then-rate pair counts once, at its rating. Named option `borrowWeight` on `ContentRecommender`, not an inline constant | A borrow is a mild positive signal, a bit above neutral (3) and below a rating of 4. 3.5 is also the suggested implicit rating for Pearson (Open #1), so one value can serve both if that is accepted. Initial value, to be revisited with the sweep if it matters. Known limitation: low ratings (1–2) still pull the profile towards the book, as in the report's plain weighted mean |
| 2026-10-13 | The harness fits TF-IDF (vocabulary and IDF) once over the **whole catalogue's text**, not per seed. Only the patron profile and popularity counts use training interactions alone | Item text is catalogue metadata with no interaction information, so nothing about the held-out interactions leaks; the held-out books must be scorable as candidates, which needs their vectors in the same vocabulary. Identical vectors are also what the app serves |
| 2026-10-13 | Sanity baseline "most popular": rank books by the number of distinct training patrons who interacted with them (ties by book id), excluding the patron's own training books. Labelled as a non-personalised sanity check, **not part of the hypothesis**, and kept apart from the content/collaborative/hybrid comparison in the output | Shows that the personalised methods beat a trivial reference |
| 2026-10-13 | Metrics are computed per patron and averaged within each segment, then mean ± sample standard deviation (n − 1) across the five seeds. Results are written to `results/eval-<configs>.json` and `.csv` | Matches CLAUDE.md FR12 |
| 2026-10-13 | **Pearson uses explicit (real) ratings only (Q1, closes Open #1).** Borrow-only books get no implicit rating in CF. They still count toward the patron's distinct-book count (so toward the cold-start threshold) and toward the content profile (at `borrowWeight` 3.5) | Simulated borrows were generated from each patron's genre preferences, so turning them into ratings would invent taste signal and favour CF and CBF artificially. Consequence to report: a patron whose training history is borrow-only, or who has fewer than 3 co-rated books with anyone, gets no CF recommendations |
| 2026-10-13 | Cycle 2 confirmations: `borrowWeight` stays 3.5; TF-IDF is fitted once over the catalogue text; the content profile stays the **plain rating-weighted mean** as specified. Adjusting it (e.g. centring ratings so low ratings push away from a book) after seeing test results would be tuning on test data | Keeps the protocol clean. Future work: rating-centred content profile, chosen on a validation split rather than the test partition |
| 2026-10-13 | **Collaborative engine parameters (fixed, not tuned against test results):** minimum overlap 3 co-rated books; k = 30 positive neighbours; Pearson computed on the co-rated books with means taken over the co-rated books (classic formulation); undefined (skipped) when either side has zero variance on the overlap; only similarity > 0 kept; ties broken by ascending user id. Prediction `pred(u,i) = r̄_u + Σ sim(u,v)·(r_v,i − r̄_v) / Σ|sim(u,v)|` over the neighbours that rated i, where r̄ is the mean of **all** the user's training explicit ratings (as in CLAUDE.md); not clamped to 1–5. Candidates must be rated by at least one neighbour; rank by predicted rating, ties by book id. The target patron is excluded from their own neighbourhood | Standard user-based CF. Not clamping avoids creating artificial ties in the ranking. Known weakness: a book rated by a single neighbour can get an extreme prediction (no shrinkage / support threshold, as the spec does not ask for one) |
| 2026-10-13 | **No backfilling in CF.** If CF yields fewer than N recommendations, the list is returned short (no padding with popular or content results). Precision@N still divides by N, so a short list is penalised. The harness reports **coverage** per segment: the share of evaluated patrons who receive 0, 1–9 or 10 recommendations | Backfilling would blur which technique produced a hit and would hide the cold-start weakness the hybrid is meant to fix |

| 2026-10-13 | **Cycle 3 confirmations:** (1) the collaborative engine keeps the co-rated-means Pearson as built. (2) **No support threshold** (minimum number of neighbours behind a prediction) is added. Adding one after seeing the results would be tuning on test data, so it is recorded as a **limitation**: a book rated by a single neighbour can get an extreme prediction. (3) **Supplementary metric:** Precision@N (and Recall/F1) over *covered* patrons only (those who received ≥ 1 recommendation), reported per segment **separately from the headline numbers**, which keep the all-evaluated-patrons denominator | Keeps the protocol clean. The covered-patron figure shows how good CF is when it can speak at all, without hiding that it often cannot |
| 2026-10-13 | **What counts toward the 3-interaction threshold (Q2, closes Open #2):** the number of **distinct books** in the patron's history, whether borrowed or rated (a borrow and a rating of the same book count once). Below `coldStartThreshold` (3): content-based only, and the collaborative recommender is **not invoked at all**. At 3 or more: weighted combination. This is the same count the evaluation uses for its cold segment, so rule and segment agree. A patron with ≥ 3 books but no co-rated neighbours gets the content ranking (see Q3) | Matches the 2026-10-13 cold-segment decision; counting distinct books stops one book from counting twice |
| 2026-10-13 | **Item cold-start (Q3, closes Open #3):** a candidate with no CF prediction contributes 0 from CF, so `final = contentWeight × A_norm`. A patron with no CF neighbours therefore gets exactly the content ranking | The simplest rule, and the SPEC §8 Q6 default. Known effect: an unrated book can never beat a book of equal content score that has a good CF prediction, and one with a poor prediction (B_norm near 0) ties with it |
| 2026-10-13 | **Normalisation (FR7):** Score A is min–max scaled **per patron over their own candidates** (the books they have not interacted with): `(a − min) / (max − min)`, and 0 for every candidate when `max = min`. Score B is **clamped to [1, 5]**, then `(B − 1) / 4` (a fixed scale, since predictions are on the rating scale and are not clamped by the CF engine). Both are then in [0, 1] | A is a cosine whose spread differs a lot between patrons, so it is rescaled per patron. B has a natural fixed range, so a fixed map keeps a prediction of 4.5 meaning the same for every patron |
| 2026-10-13 | **Attribution (per recommended item):** `contentContribution = contentWeight × A_norm`, `collabContribution = collabWeight × B_norm`. The item's source is `CONTENT` if the content contribution is **≥** the CF contribution, otherwise `COLLABORATIVE`. Every cold-path item is `CONTENT`. The harness reports the share of recommended items per source, per segment | Gives the attribution analysis a deterministic rule that can be written to `RecommendationLog.sourceComponent` in Cycle 5. Ties (including both being 0) go to CONTENT |
| 2026-10-13 | **Weight tuning protocol:** weights are chosen on a **validation split carved from each seed's training data only**, never the test set. The validation split reuses `splitInteractions` (same pair-level rules: 20% of distinct books, ≥ 2 kept for training, same relevance rule) on that seed's training rows, with seed `1000 + seed`. Grid: `contentWeight` 0.0 to 1.0 in steps of 0.1, `collabWeight = 1 − contentWeight`. The chosen weight is the one with the highest **mean validation F1@10 over all evaluated patrons, averaged over seeds 1–5** (ties: the lower `contentWeight`). The chosen weights are then evaluated **once** on the test sets. 0.35/0.65 stays in the final comparison as the pre-registered initial setting | Keeps the test partition untouched by tuning. Note that a patron in the cold segment is unaffected by the weights (content only), and in validation a patron needs ≥ 3 training books to have any book held out |
| 2026-10-13 | At `contentWeight = 0` the hybrid ranks by B_norm alone, and candidates with no CF prediction all tie at 0, so the rest of the list is filled in book-id order (the engine's usual tie-break). This is an endpoint of the sweep, not a sensible operating point, and the hybrid always returns a full list (the content scores supply every candidate), unlike CF alone | The rule follows directly from Q3 and the fixed tie-break; stated so the sweep's left end is read correctly |

## Report wording

> Interactions were partitioned at the level of user–book pairs, holding out approximately 20% of each patron's pairs while retaining at least two for training. A held-out item was treated as relevant if the patron's real rating was 4 or above; simulated borrow events were used for training only.
